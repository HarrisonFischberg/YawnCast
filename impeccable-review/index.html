Option 1 · Lead direction

Boro

The bed itself: indigo-dyed futon cloth, mended with white sashiko running stitch. It is the most physical answer to the brief's "carefully designed listening object", and it is dark without being a night sky. The running stitch becomes the system: the scrubber is a stitched line, played episodes get a stitch through them instead of disappearing, and the Moon key is a persimmon patch whose ten stitches are the ten fade steps. Every show is a patch sewn onto the cloth at one fixed scale.

Key changes (iOS)

  • Starfield and waves removed; a still woven indigo ground on every screen
  • Pager dots become labelled New / Library / Explore with a stitched underline
  • Scrubber, dividers, progress and key outlines drawn as running stitch
  • Moon key is a persimmon patch: "Sweet dreams" with heart, "Fade in 17 minutes.", −5 / ten stitches / +5
  • Display type: Shippori Mincho; UI stays SF Pro
  • Day mode is pale first-dip indigo, not cream or white

Stays the same

  • Three pages, Show, Now Playing, mini player on every screen
  • Physical keys with travel and haptics
  • Owner's Sleep copy, ±5, Sleep timing
  • Publisher artwork unmodified

Honest risk

Indigo is still blue light at bedtime, and a textile world can tip into craft-fair decoration if the stitch shows up anywhere it isn't doing a job.

Raised by the declined challengers

  • Ticket wallet: nothing disappears, it cancels. Played episodes are stitched through, not hidden.
  • Botanical folio: one fixed scale and ground for every show patch.
  • Timetable rack: rank by weight, not size, so rows survive AX5.
Option 2 · My pick

Almanac

The farmer's almanac: goldenrod cover, letterpress slab type, ruled tables, and a column of moon phases. The Moon key already carries the product, so this direction makes the fade literal: ten steps are ten phases, from full moon at full volume to new moon at pause. At night the page goes ink-black with goldenrod reserved for the Moon; by day the whole screen is almanac yellow. Episodes sit in a dated table, and played ones are struck through rather than removed.

Key changes (iOS)

  • Masthead with the date and the live "Fade at 23:58" time
  • Pages as ruled tabs; Library as a ruled two-column table
  • Show episodes in a date column, played rows struck through
  • Moon key shows the current phase large and all ten phases below
  • Display: Alfa Slab One and Old Standard; UI stays SF Pro
  • Day mode is a committed goldenrod field with a red Moon

Stays the same

  • All screens and navigation model
  • Sleep timing and ±5 (only +5 shown while fading)
  • Owner's "Ready for bed?" copy
  • Artwork unmodified

Honest risk

It is the most familiar of the five: a moon motif is where most sleep apps land, and it sits closest to today's Midnight theme. The day yellow is loud by design.

Option 3 · Challenger, fused

Safelight

A darkroom under the red safelight, where nothing on screen emits blue light. The whole palette is an eleven-step warm zone ramp, which happens to be exactly the fade: 100%, 90% … 0% in ten steps. Now Playing shows that ramp with the current step outlined in the lit amber of the Moon key, so you can read how far the fade has gone at a glance. Condensed caps carry labels and tabular figures carry time; by day the ramp turns into neutral print greys.

Key changes (iOS)

  • One eleven-step zone ramp replaces ten separate themes for night
  • Fade shown as the ramp with "Step 4 of 10 · 60%"
  • Condensed caps for titles and labels (Barlow Condensed); SF Pro for reading
  • Square-cornered keys and sheets, no shadows
  • Day mode: paper greys with a deep red Moon

Stays the same

  • Screens, navigation, Sleep logic, ±5
  • Owner's Sleep copy
  • Artwork unmodified (it is the only blue left on screen)

Honest risk

Few listeners know a darkroom, so the zone ramp has to be legible as "volume" on its own. Today's Red Light theme already lives near here; this makes it the whole product.

Verdict: competitive. It wins on product clarity (the ramp is the fade) and trails on audience recognition.

Option 4 · Grounded alternate

Sign-Off

Late-night radio, where podcasts come from: deep after-hours plum, QSL-card show tiles, wide heavy call-letter type, and a red on-air lamp for the Moon. Sleep is the station signing off for the night: ten broadcast colour bars go dark one at a time as the volume falls. The scrubber becomes a tuning scale with a red needle. Day mode is a pale lilac studio with the same lamp.

Key changes (iOS)

  • Pages become a native segmented control
  • Shows as QSL cards with a caption band
  • Moon key is an on-air lamp with −5, ten colour bars, +5
  • Scrubber drawn as a tuning ruler with a red needle
  • Display: Archivo Expanded; UI stays SF Pro

Stays the same

  • Screens, Sleep logic, owner's copy on the key
  • Mini player on every screen (now a capsule)
  • Artwork unmodified

Honest risk

The colour bars bring cyan and blue into a bedtime screen, and the wide caps are loud for a half-asleep reader. Copy like "Signs off in 17 min" changes the app's voice.

Option 5 · Grounded alternate

Picture Book

The bedtime story's painted room: flat gouache greens, a red curtain, a yellow lamp, cut-paper shapes with slightly uneven corners. Now Playing gets a small painted window and lamp, and the fade is the lamp's glow shrinking step by step until the room is dark and the episode pauses. Shows sit on painted mats in red, mustard and blue. By day the green brightens and the window shows the sun.

Key changes (iOS)

  • Deep bedroom green replaces navy; no stars
  • Painted window and lamp on Now Playing that dims with each step
  • Coloured mats behind artwork, star marks new episodes
  • Soft, uneven "cut paper" corners on keys and rows
  • Display: Young Serif; UI stays SF Pro

Stays the same

  • Screens, navigation, Sleep timing, ±5
  • Owner's "Ready for bed?" copy
  • Artwork unmodified; no large episode art on Now Playing

Honest risk

It can read as a children's app, which matters for listeners of history and true-crime shows. The illustration adds the one large drawn element the brief otherwise keeps off Now Playing.

Challengers considered and set aside
  • Jet-age ticket wallet (declined): kept its rule that used things cancel rather than vanish, now in Boro's stitched-through episodes.
  • Botanical folio (declined): kept its fixed scale and ground for every plate, now Boro's equal show patches.
  • Timetable slide rack (declined): kept rank-by-weight-not-size, which helps the AX5 layouts.
  • Convention catalog grid (declined): its whole-cell grid repacking is already how the Small layout works.
  • Record-sleeve catalog (competitive, held back): matte black and one plot owning the field; Safelight covers the same darkness more usefully.
